# #381 item 7: what is and is not measured

Item 7 of #381 reads: "Jolt snapshot cost and Jolt-vs-PhysX speed are unmeasured; thread-count independence of Jolt is unproven
(preset stays single-threaded); the determinism corpus has only 10 scenes." This note separates the four claims into measured and not.
Sources: `orchestrator/proposals/physics-bench-381.md` (PR #385, the only Jolt/PhysX timings), `physx-determinism-measured-numbers.md`
(PR #161, PhysX only, hashes not speed), `tests/engine/physics/PhysicsBackendBenchTests.cpp`, `JoltSnapshotTests.cpp`,
`PhysicsDeterminismCorpusTests.cpp`, `scripts/physics-determinism.py`, `.github/workflows/tests.yml` and
`engine/src/physics/jolt/JoltBackend.cpp`. No benchmark was run for this note; every number is quoted.

Where the numbers came from matters: the #385 timings are **one run on one Windows 10 machine, MSVC Release, strict FP off, both
backends single-threaded**, printed by a test that asserts nothing about speed. They are **not printed by any CI leg**: no workflow step
greps `[BENCH]` (the only physics steps that print are "Jolt determinism numbers", "Box2D determinism numbers", "Jolt backend
determinism numbers" and "PhysX determinism numbers", `tests.yml:264-315` and the equivalents). ctest hides a passing test's output, so the
bench lines exist only in the PR text and in `physics-bench-381.md`.

## What this means for the issue

- The "unproven thread-count independence" claim is stale for the tested scene and should be reworded to "proven on one scene, not
  on the corpus".
- Snapshot cost and Jolt vs PhysX are measured once, on one machine, on settled piles. They support the statements "a restore is
  about 0.8 of a step and does not limit the rollback window" and "Jolt and PhysX are within about 25% at 100 to 400 bodies" and
  nothing stronger. They say nothing about the web player, where the browser step cost of the backend decides playability.
- The corpus grows only through the Linux golden path; that, not the test code, is the real cost of adding a scene.
- The cheapest improvement to all of this is one CI step that prints the bench into the job summary on the Linux, arm64 and web-node
  legs. That makes the numbers reproducible and comparable, and removes the "one Windows machine" caveat, without a local run.
